# Science and Engineering Primer for the Compute-Verified Solver

This primer grounds the deterministic relations the standalone science solver
may emit. Every relation is stated in words so an ontology check can support
the claim before a calculation is emitted.

## Kinematics

Speed equals distance divided by time. Distance equals speed multiplied by
time. Acceleration equals the change of velocity divided by time. Final
velocity equals initial velocity plus acceleration multiplied by time.

## Dynamics and energy

Force equals mass multiplied by acceleration. Weight equals mass multiplied
by gravitational acceleration. Work equals force multiplied by distance.
Power equals work divided by time. Kinetic energy equals one half mass
multiplied by velocity squared. Momentum equals mass multiplied by velocity.

## Thermodynamics

Heat equals mass multiplied by specific heat capacity multiplied by the
change of temperature. Efficiency equals useful work divided by total work.

## Matter and solutions

Density equals mass divided by volume. Molarity equals amount of substance
divided by volume. Amount of substance equals mass divided by molar mass.
Dilution conserves the amount of solute, so concentration times volume is
constant. The ideal gas pressure equals amount times the gas constant times
temperature divided by volume.

## Conservation

In a perfectly inelastic collision momentum is conserved, so the common
velocity equals the total initial momentum divided by the total mass.

## Combinatorics and statistics

A binomial coefficient counts the number of ways to choose k items from n
items without replacement. A confidence interval half width equals the
critical value multiplied by the standard deviation divided by the square
root of the sample size.

## Tabular data

The arithmetic mean of a measured column is the sum of its values divided by
the number of values. The least squares slope of a measured series equals the
covariance of the abscissa and the ordinate divided by the variance of the
abscissa.

## Units

The base dimensions are mass, length, time, thermodynamic temperature, amount
of substance and electric current. The metre, centimetre, millimetre and
kilometre measure length. The second, millisecond, minute and hour measure
time. The kilogram, gram and milligram measure mass. The newton measures
force. The joule measures energy and work. The watt measures power. The
pascal measures pressure. The kelvin measures thermodynamic temperature. The
mole measures amount of substance. The litre and millilitre measure volume.
