# Science and Engineering Primer for the Compute-Verified Solver

This primer grounds the deterministic relations the standalone science solver
may emit. Every relation is stated in words so an ontology check can support
the claim before a calculation is emitted.

## Kinematics

Speed equals distance divided by time. Distance equals speed multiplied by
time. Acceleration equals the change of velocity divided by time. Final
velocity equals initial velocity plus acceleration multiplied by time.

## Dynamics and energy

Force equals mass multiplied by acceleration. Weight equals mass multiplied
by gravitational acceleration. Work equals force multiplied by distance.
Gravitational potential work equals mass multiplied by gravitational
acceleration multiplied by height. Elastic spring work equals one half spring
constant multiplied by displacement squared. Power equals work divided by
time. Kinetic energy equals one half mass multiplied by velocity squared.
Momentum equals mass multiplied by velocity. Reduced mass equals the product
of two masses divided by their sum.

## Thermodynamics

Heat equals mass multiplied by specific heat capacity multiplied by the
change of temperature. Isothermal entropy change equals reversible heat
divided by temperature. Efficiency equals useful work divided by total work.

## Matter and solutions

Density equals mass divided by volume. Molarity equals amount of substance
divided by volume. Amount of substance equals mass divided by molar mass.
Dilution conserves the amount of solute, so concentration times volume is
constant. The ideal gas pressure equals amount times the gas constant times
temperature divided by volume. The ideal gas pressure from sample mass equals
mass divided by molar mass times the gas constant times temperature divided by
volume. The ideal gas volume equals amount times the gas constant times
temperature divided by pressure. The ideal gas volume from sample mass equals
mass divided by molar mass times the gas constant times temperature divided by
pressure. The ideal gas temperature equals pressure times volume divided by
amount times the gas constant. The ideal gas amount equals pressure times
volume divided by the gas constant times temperature. At constant temperature
ideal gas pressure times volume is constant. At constant pressure ideal gas
volume is proportional to absolute temperature. At constant volume ideal gas
pressure is proportional to absolute temperature. Root mean square gas speed
equals the square root of three times the gas constant times temperature
divided by molar mass.

## Radiation and quantum mechanics

Electromagnetic wave frequency equals the speed of light divided by wavelength.
Electromagnetic wavelength equals the speed of light divided by frequency.
Photon energy equals Planck constant multiplied by frequency. Photon energy
equals Planck constant multiplied by the speed of light divided by wavelength.
Photon wavelength equals Planck constant multiplied by the speed of light
divided by energy. Molar photon energy equals Avogadro constant times Planck
constant times the speed of light divided by wavelength. Blackbody temperature
equals the Wien displacement constant divided by peak wavelength. Peak
blackbody wavelength equals the Wien displacement constant divided by
temperature. De Broglie wavelength equals Planck constant divided by mass
multiplied by velocity. De Broglie wavelength from kinetic energy equals
Planck constant divided by the square root of two times mass times kinetic
energy.

## Electromagnetism

Electrostatic work equals electric charge multiplied by potential difference.
Electrostatic force equals Coulomb constant times the product of charge
magnitudes divided by distance squared. Separation between point charges
equals the square root of Coulomb constant times the product of charge
magnitudes divided by force. Point charge electric field equals Coulomb
constant times charge magnitude divided by distance squared. Electric force in
a uniform electric field equals charge magnitude multiplied by electric field.
Acceleration in a uniform electric field equals charge magnitude multiplied by
electric field divided by mass. Electric dipole moment equals charge magnitude
multiplied by separation. Net electric flux through a closed surface equals
enclosed charge divided by vacuum permittivity. Elementary charge count equals
total charge magnitude divided by the elementary charge. Electric current
equals voltage divided by resistance. Electric potential difference equals
current multiplied by resistance. Electrical power equals voltage multiplied by
current.

## Conservation

In a perfectly inelastic collision momentum is conserved, so the common
velocity equals the total initial momentum divided by the total mass.

## Combinatorics, series and statistics

A binomial coefficient counts the number of ways to choose k items from n
items without replacement. The sum of a convergent infinite geometric series
equals the first term divided by one minus the common ratio. Conditional
probability equals joint probability divided by the conditioning event
probability. For independent events the joint probability equals the product
of the individual probabilities. A confidence interval half width equals the
critical value multiplied by the standard deviation divided by the square
root of the sample size.

## Tabular data

The arithmetic mean of a measured column is the sum of its values divided by
the number of values. The least squares slope of a measured series equals the
covariance of the abscissa and the ordinate divided by the variance of the
abscissa.

## Units

The base dimensions are mass, length, time, thermodynamic temperature, amount
of substance and electric current. The metre, centimetre, millimetre,
micrometre, nanometre, picometre and kilometre measure length. The second,
millisecond, minute and hour measure time, and the hertz measures frequency.
The kilogram, gram, milligram and atomic mass unit measure mass. The newton
measures force. The joule and electronvolt measure energy and work. The watt
measures power. The pascal, kilopascal, bar, atmosphere and torr measure
pressure. The kelvin measures thermodynamic temperature. The mole measures
amount of substance. The coulomb measures electric charge and the volt
measures electric potential. The litre, cubic decimetre and millilitre measure
volume.
